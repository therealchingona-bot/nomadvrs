Travel guide · San Diego Bay

The Coronado guide

Everything we tell guests on the phone about Coronado, written down: what the town is actually good at, which season does what, where to stay, and the mistakes visitors make.

Coronado is not technically an island, but it behaves like one: a bridge in, a beach that runs flat and white for a mile and a half, and a village grid you can cover on a beach cruiser in twenty minutes. It is about as family-legible as a beach town gets — no cliffs, no crowds pressed against a boardwalk, wide sidewalks, and a Main Street with a hardware store and an old movie theatre. The housing stock is cottages in the village grid and condos out at the Shores, most of it within a few blocks of sand.

Why people come here

One of the country's best beaches

Wide, gently sloped, mica-flecked sand with lifeguards, fire rings, and room to spread out even in August.

Bike everything

Flat, quiet streets and a bay-side path around the golf course and the ferry landing. Most guests never start the car.

Ten minutes from downtown

The bridge or the pedestrian ferry puts you in the Gaslamp and at Petco Park quickly, then lets you retreat.

History you can walk into

The 1888 Victorian resort, the Spreckels mansions, and North Island's aviation past are all inside the village.

The seasons, honestly

Winter (Dec–Feb). Empty beach, 64°F days, whale watching from the point. Best value of the year.

Spring (Mar–May). Mild and quiet with marine layer mornings. Shoulder rates through mid-May.

Summer (Jun–Aug). Peak season. Book six months out for beach-block cottages; expect a Saturday-to-Saturday minimum.

Fall (Sep–Nov). Warmest water of the year, clear skies, and school back in session. The local favorite.

Where to stay

Coronado Village. The walkable historic grid — Orange Avenue, the cottages, and a three-block walk to sand.

Coronado Shores. Ten oceanfront towers with pools, tennis, and direct beach access south of the resort.

Imperial Beach. The Silver Strand's south end — surfier, quieter, and better value per bedroom.

Getting here and getting around

Fly into San Diego International (SAN) — 20 min. A car is optional but useful — mountain and desert distances are not walkable, and rideshare coverage thins out badly at the edges of each market. Every Nomad home has off-street parking included in the rate, and 2 in the portfolio have EV charging.

Ask us something else
Do I need a car on Coronado?

Rarely. The village is flat and walkable, and a bike covers it end to end. You will want a car for a day trip to La Jolla or the mountains, but not for the island itself.

Is there a minimum stay in Coronado?

Coronado's short-term rental rules and the owner's own preference both set a minimum, and the two change independently. The number therefore depends on the specific home and the season rather than on the town.

How far is the beach in Coronado?

From the village grid it is a two-to-six block walk, flat the whole way — about five to fifteen minutes.
