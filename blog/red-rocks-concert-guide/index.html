The Red Rocks Show Night Guide We Give Our Own Guests

Where to park, when to leave, what the rock does to the temperature, and why staying in Evergreen usually beats staying in Denver.

By Nomad Vacation Rentals · Published June 18, 2026 · Updated July 30, 2026 · 8 min read

We have sent several hundred guests to Red Rocks shows from our Evergreen and Denver homes, and almost every problem they have ever had came down to one of three things: leaving too late, parking in the wrong lot, or dressing for the temperature at 7pm instead of the temperature at 10:30pm. None of that is in the venue's FAQ. Here is what we actually tell people.

Leave two hours before the doors, not the set

The bottleneck at Red Rocks is not the parking lots, it is the single access road up from Morrison. On a sold-out night that road is at a standstill 90 minutes before the headliner, and the last mile can take 40 minutes. From our Evergreen homes the drive is 27 minutes without traffic. Plan on 70 and you will park calmly, tailgate for an hour, and be inside for the opener. Plan on 30 and you will be angry in a line of brake lights with a great view of the sunset you are missing.

Park in an upper lot, always

General parking is free. Upper North and Upper South put you at the top of the amphitheatre, which means you walk down to your seat and climb back at the end. Lower South is closer to the road but leaves you climbing roughly 200 vertical feet of stairs before the first song, at 6,450 feet of elevation. If anyone in your group has asthma, a knee, or has flown in from sea level that day, this is the single most important sentence in this article: take the upper lot.

The rock is a battery

Those sandstone monoliths absorb heat all afternoon and radiate it until about an hour after sunset, then stop. A show that starts at 78°F routinely finishes at 45–50°F, and thunderstorms build over the foothills on summer afternoons with almost no warning. Every experienced Red Rocks regular carries the same three things: a layer, a rain shell that packs down, and more water than seems reasonable. The venue allows one sealed factory water bottle per person most nights, and there are free filling stations inside.

Rideshare is the expensive way to do this

Pickup is staged well down the access road, which means a walk of a mile or more in the dark, into 2–3x surge pricing, with 9,000 other people requesting at once. If you are staying somewhere with a car and a designated driver, drive. If you are staying in Denver and drinking, the shuttle services that run from LoDo and RiNo are far better value than a rideshare round trip.

Evergreen or Denver?

Both work, for different trips. Evergreen is 25–30 minutes west: quiet, pines, a hot tub to get into at midnight, and no traffic on the way home because you are driving away from the city. Denver's RiNo or LoDo is 25–45 minutes east: dinner before, bars after, and a shuttle or rideshare so nobody drives. The honest split we see is that couples and small groups prefer Evergreen, and bachelor parties and multi-show weekends prefer Denver.

Go back in the morning

The best Red Rocks experience is free. On non-event days the amphitheatre is open to the public from sunrise until an hour after sunset — you can walk the rows, run the stairs with the local fitness crowd, and hike the 1.4-mile Trading Post loop below the venue. Get there for sunrise the morning after a show and you will have the place nearly to yourself, with the Denver skyline lighting up 6,000 feet below.

The short version

  • Leave two hours before doors on a sold-out night, 90 minutes midweek.
  • Park Upper North or Upper South. Always.
  • Layers and a rain shell, regardless of the afternoon forecast.
  • Water at altitude: a liter before the show, a liter during.
  • Check the current bag policy the week of the show — it changes.
  • Come back at sunrise on a non-event day. It is better than the concert.
